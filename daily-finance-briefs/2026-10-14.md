## 2. Daily finance lesson

*Category 1 of 4: Foundational budgeting and banking*

**The concept: real interest rate.** Your real interest rate is what your savings earn after inflation, like walking up a down escalator: your bank rate is your walking speed, and inflation is the escalator pulling you back.

```
Real rate  ≈  Bank interest rate  -  Inflation rate
```

**Why it matters:**

- Today's report says prices are up 3.7% in a year. Money earning less than 3.7% buys less each year, even while the dollar number grows.
- When the Fed raises rates, banks compete for deposits and pay more. Moving cash from a low-rate account to a high-yield one can turn a negative real rate positive.

**The visual breakdown:**

```
THE ESCALATOR (inflation = 3.7% per year)

  Big-bank savings          High-yield savings
  you walk up   +0.5%       you walk up   +4.5%
  escalator     -3.7%       escalator     -3.7%
  ─────────────────────     ─────────────────────
  real rate    ≈ -3.2%      real rate    ≈ +0.8%
  you slide backward        you climb slowly


BUYING POWER OF $10,000 AFTER 5 YEARS (in today's dollars)
each █ = $250

Start             ████████████████████████████████████████    $10,000
Cash at home 0%   █████████████████████████████████           $8,339  (-17%)
Big bank   0.5%   ██████████████████████████████████          $8,549  (-15%)
High-yield 4.5%   ██████████████████████████████████████████  $10,392  (+4%)
```

The 0.5% and 4.5% rates are examples, not live quotes. Before moving money, check that the account is FDIC-insured (US government protection up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank).

> **The golden rule:** Beat inflation or your savings shrink.
