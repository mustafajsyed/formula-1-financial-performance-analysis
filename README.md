# Formula 1 Team Financial Performance Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes the relationship between Formula 1 team financial strength and on-track performance. It compares 2025 Constructors’ Championship results with 2024 revenue estimates and 2025 estimated team valuations for all 10 Formula 1 teams.

The purpose of the project was to develop practical Microsoft Excel, financial analysis, data visualization, and business communication skills. The analysis examines whether teams with higher revenues and valuations necessarily achieve stronger racing results.

## Project Objective

The main question explored was:

**Which Formula 1 team produced the strongest sporting performance relative to its financial scale?**

The project compares teams using:

- Constructors’ Championship points
- Race wins
- Podium finishes
- Estimated revenue
- Estimated team valuation
- Revenue per Constructors’ point
- Constructors’ points per $100 million of revenue

## Methodology

Sporting-performance data was collected for the 2025 Formula 1 season. Revenue figures represent estimated 2024 operations, while team valuations are 2025 estimates.

The data was organized in Excel and used to calculate two performance-to-revenue measures:

### Revenue per Constructors’ Point

This measures how much revenue a team generated for every championship point it scored. A lower result indicates stronger sporting performance relative to revenue.

### Constructors’ Points per $100 Million of Revenue

This measures how many championship points a team earned for every $100 million in estimated revenue. A higher result indicates stronger performance relative to revenue.

These calculations do not measure profit, actual spending, or return on investment.

## Key Findings

- McLaren recorded the strongest sporting performance with 833 Constructors’ Championship points.
- McLaren also earned the most race wins, with 14 victories.
- McLaren generated approximately 135.7 championship points per $100 million of revenue, the highest result in the analysis.
- Red Bull Racing ranked second in revenue efficiency with approximately 73.0 points per $100 million of revenue.
- Ferrari had the highest estimated team valuation at approximately $6.5 billion.
- Mercedes had the second-highest estimated valuation at approximately $6.0 billion.
- Alpine recorded the lowest sporting-performance efficiency, producing approximately 7.3 points per $100 million of revenue.
- Higher revenue and team valuation did not automatically result in stronger racing performance.

## Main Conclusion

The analysis suggests that financial strength alone does not determine Formula 1 success. Ferrari and Mercedes were among the most valuable teams, but McLaren produced substantially stronger sporting results relative to its revenue.

This indicates that factors such as engineering, strategy, driver performance, management, and the effective use of financial resources can influence competitive success.

## Limitations

The sporting results are from the 2025 season, while the revenue estimates represent 2024 operations. Team valuations are 2025 estimates and are not official audited financial statements.

Revenue is not the same as spending, profit, operating cost, or investment. Therefore, the efficiency calculations should be interpreted as performance-to-revenue comparisons rather than measures of profitability or return on investment.
